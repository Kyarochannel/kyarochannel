Recently I have finished the latest Genshin update released by Hoyo, and it brought a lot of thoughts to me, about the general story itself, the characters, our main protagonist and the general game direction. In general it left me quite polarized. This post will contain spoilers from 7.2 and earlier story already released in the game.

The latest update starts off pretty well. The tensions are high, Ronova just did something to Snezhnayan's Nail of Retribution, and it started affecting Nails across the other nations, besides only Fontaine (because there's no nail). We witness the meeting of the archons, as well as encounter many of Traveler's old friends. This is quite nice, but one thing I noticed is that I started getting annoyed by Paimon's constant narration.

To some this may be a surprise, since hate on Paimon's constant yapping has probably been present since the game released 6 years ago, yet up till now I didn't mind it that much. I think this may have a lot to do with the fact that Hoyo has started to selectively give our Traveler more voiced lines, and this patch, honestly, there weren't a lot of them, at least for me. Generally this patch could've played out without Traveler, as they were mainly helping on the sidelines or witnessing events play out, led by the main characters of 7.2.

However, let's move on to the defense of Teyvat itself. This war, or large scale event that spanned our entire map, has some ups and downs. On one hand, it was really fun to revisit the old nations and feel some nostalgia, as well as meet many — and I mean many — characters. On the other, the stuff we actually did felt more like the seasonal minigames Hoyo throws at players every now and then, because they were essentially reused minigames from older events.

And that wasn't terrible, they made sense in the story, but after some time they felt more like a chore. Most of the lore in that part was hidden in descriptions of different teams doing various things across the nations. Personally I most enjoyed the small encounters in Fontaine, as they really did a good job at showcasing the tension and slowly growing despair — ironically in the only nation not struck by the nail.

The final part of the archon quest is probably where I felt most conflicted. Entering Celestia and witnessing Nukvah, the advanced intelligence which is essentially Celestia's computer, was cool, and the revelation of the doom impending despite completing the trial of usurpation was an emotional one. However, what lacked, at least for me, was Traveler themselves.

It seems like we're in a weird limbo state where one patch they make a step forward to gain actual agency, stepping forward and actually acting like a 4th descender could be expected to, only to fall back into being the complacent witness of all that's happening — probably because the 5 stars need to sell well. I understand that Hoyo probably doesn't want to make Traveler much more than a witness; it was hinted at many times, and if they want to actually push them forward as a proactive main protagonist, it will be at a snail's pace either way.

To me it simply feels a bit disappointing that an interesting character with a rich background makes so little progress in the 6 year long story. It is safe and proven, but I feel like we may eventually run into a wall. Another thing that worries me is that in the climax of the 7 nation part of Genshin's story, Traveler may simply be left out so that actually sellable characters, like the upcoming Tsaritsa, will sell really well.

Moving to the general game itself, the update has probably sparked most criticism around the new open-world areas of Snezhnaya. In that case I must agree with the popular opinion that this time calling the new areas "open-world" may be a stretch, because it's basically a really small mass of land severely limited by the sheer cold mechanics not letting us go anywhere, and quickly becoming incredibly annoying.

Yeah, the accessible land itself is beautiful, and the accompanying music even more so, but requiring us to simply teleport to new areas by a pre-unlocked waypoint isn't how an open-world should work. At the start of 7.2 we're even told we can go there by train, but guess what… we can't. The area is locked till you teleport to the first waypoint, and only then the game unlocks the railroad to new areas.

This in a way is a culmination of the weird map development Hoyo chose since Natlan: narrow corridors detached from the rest of the world, not really accessible by foot in some cases. And I must say I don't love that. What I enjoyed as much as the story in the early days of Genshin was the incredible open world — it was essentially Breath of the Wild, but outside of Nintendo's walled garden, for me, and for free. The slow trek from Mondstadt to Liyue was an essential experience for all players, and most likely quite enjoyed by most of them.

Many people argue that this direction may be because Hoyo plans to add a lot of new terrain much later on, simply leaving space to work with, but only time can tell. Among the beautiful music, gripping story and yet very few cutscenes, this update was like a roller-coaster to me, with lots of ups and downs.

I guess only 7.3 will let me honestly judge it in my heart — was it better than the current update? Did I judge it too harshly, or was it adequate criticism? For now, I'll sit with the uncertainty, and wait to find out.
